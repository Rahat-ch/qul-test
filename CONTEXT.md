# Context

Vocabulary for the QUL developer-relations project: a Quran reading prototype built on the Quranic Universal Library, plus the developer-advocacy plan it informs.

## Glossary

### Builder
A person who makes software for Muslims, the audience the developer-relations function serves. Preferred over "Muslim builder", "Islamic product developer", and "developer" when referring to the people we support.

### Project
Something a Builder makes: an app, library, dataset tool, or service. A Project may consume one or more Resources.

### Resource
A dataset published by QUL: a translation, tafsir, transliteration, recitation, script, font, mushaf layout, or metadata set. Each Resource carries its own license.

### Program
Anything Tarteel runs for Builders: grants, mentorship, showcases, office hours, hackathons. A Program allocates Grants.

### Grant
Any Tarteel resource allocated to a Project or Builder through a Program: money, mentorship hours, design time, or credits.

### Cash Grant
A Grant whose allocation is money. The only kind of Grant that needs a budget line.

### Friction
A logged moment where a Builder gets stuck or slowed while working with QUL. Each Friction is attributed to one of the five objectives it bears on.

### Objective
One of the five goals Tarteel set for the developer-relations role, quoted from the interview brief. Referenced by number (Objective 1 through 5); the one-word shorthand is ours.

1. Scaffolding: "Save other Muslim builders from wasting time on basic Quranic resource collection and scaffolding, allowing them to focus on delivering unique value."
2. Unblocking: "Unblock the progress of valuable projects where small-scale funding or mentorship are the blocker."
3. Community: "Build a community and culture of support for Islamic product developers."
4. Direction: "Encourage Muslim builders to tackle problems faced by the ummah, especially within the scope of the Quran and Islamic sciences."
5. Talent: "Identify and connect with builders that align with our mission while also being the best at their craft."

### Spread
One screen of the reading prototype, modeled on an open book: the reading page (Arabic with transliteration) and the facing page (translation with tafsir) for the same run of ayahs.

### Reading Page
The page of a Spread that shows each ayah in Arabic followed by its transliteration, with recitation controls.

### Facing Page
The page of a Spread that shows the English translation and tafsir for the same ayahs as the Reading Page.
