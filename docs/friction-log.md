# Friction Log

One entry per Friction met while building the prototype on QUL, in the order met. Severity: low (annoyance), medium (cost real time), high (blocked or nearly gave up). Objective numbers refer to the five role objectives.

| # | Step | Expected | What happened | Severity | Time lost | Objective |
|---|------|----------|---------------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Download any Resource | Click "Download json" and get a file | Signed out, every download link 302s to the sign-in page. An account is mandatory before a Builder can see a single byte of data. Nothing on the resource page says so. | medium | 5 min | 1 |
| 2 | Pick the Indopak script | One obvious Indopak ayah-by-ayah Resource | Three ayah-by-ayah Indopak variants (Indopak, Indopak Nastaleeq, Digital Khatt Indopak) with no description of how they differ or which font each needs. Chose the one whose page lists a related font. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
| 3 | Check the script's license | A license line on the resource page | No license, copyright, or permission statement anywhere on the page. Same for every Resource in this run. The FAQ says to check "dataset-specific licensing details" that do not exist. | high | 15 min | 2 |
| 4 | Read the script JSON | Plain Unicode text | Every ayah ends with a private-use-area character that renders as a box in any font except the matched QUL font. Not mentioned on the page. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 5 | Get the Indopak font | Copy the @font-face from the docs | The example @font-face has an empty font-family, and the CDN path in it says "Hanafi" where "Hafs" is meant (the tag on the page says Hafs). The path works, but it looks wrong. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 6 | Pick a transliteration | A Resource named for its source | Seven English-ish variants named "Transliteration", "English Transliteration(Tajweed)", "(RTF Updated)", "Syllables 1/2/(Old)". No author, no source, no sample beyond one ayah. Picked by reading previews. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
| 7 | Pick a translation download | One json file | Saheeh International offers eight download variants (simple, chunks, inline footnotes, footnote tags, each in json and sqlite) with no explanation of the differences. Guessed "simple". | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 8 | Read the tafsir JSON | One object per ayah | Values are mixed types: grouped ayahs are objects with an ayah_keys list, and the ayahs they cover are bare strings pointing at the group key ("2:4": "2:3"). A naive `entry.text` read crashes on 20 ayahs. Ten entries also contain HTML. | medium | 15 min | 1 |
| 9 | Find the recitation Resource | One Alafasy entry | Four Alafasy entries (411 surah-by-surah, 118 ayah-by-ayah, 414 "Streaming", 327 with English). "Streaming" is never explained. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 10 | Read the segments JSON | Ayah start and end times | Segments are word-level triples [wordPosition, startMs, endMs] with ayah bounds in timestamp_from/to. Units are only inferable from the values. Thirty ayahs start before the previous ayah ends, and the audio for surahs 2 to 114 has no bismillah, which an ayah-highlighting player has to know. | medium | 15 min | 1 |
| 11 | Find the reciter's name in the data | A reciter field in surah.json | surah.json has only surah_number, audio_url, duration. The reciter's name lives only on the catalog page and has to be hard-coded. | low | 2 min | 1 |
| 12 | Follow the docs nav | "Downloading & Using Data" opens | The nav link goes to /docs/downloading-and-using-data, which is a 404 quiz page. The real page is /docs/downloading-data. | low | 3 min | 1 |
| 13 | Check bundle size | Small text bundle | 7.7 MB for all text Resources plus font; the tafsir is 1.8 MB and segments 2.1 MB. Acceptable for a server-read bundle, too large to ship to a browser wholesale. | low | 0 | 1 |
| 14 | Render a standalone bismillah | A bismillah string somewhere in the Resources | No Resource carries it as its own field. The line has to be lifted out of ayah 1:1 of the script Resource and have its ayah-end ornament stripped by hand. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
| 15 | Strip the ayah-end ornament | One trailing marker character per ayah | It is U+06DF plus one private-use codepoint on 6029 ayahs, two private-use codepoints on 206, and on 22:77 alone the order is reversed. No mapping is published, so the only safe strip is a character class over the whole private-use block. | medium | 20 min | 1 |
| 16 | Apply the bismillah rule | A documented metadata field | surah-names.json has `bismillah_pre`, false for both At-Tawbah and Al-Fatihah. The name reads as "this surah has a bismillah", which is wrong for Al-Fatihah, where it is ayah 1. Nothing in the download or on the catalog page defines the field; that it happens to match the reader's rule was only confirmed by inspection. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
| 17 | Assert on the Arabic in a test | Typing an ayah's opening words matches the data | The Indopak Resource's diacritic codepoints differ from what a keyboard produces for the same words, so hand-typed Arabic never string-matches. Test literals had to be rebuilt from the file's own codepoints. | low | 10 min | 1 |
| 18 | Read the transliteration | Plain Latin transliteration | Entries follow an undocumented convention: parenthesised case endings ("Qaiyoom,(u)"), inconsistent hyphenation ("Wa-minan naasi mainy yaqoolu ... wa-bil-yawmil"), and letter-name spellings ("Alif-Laam-Meeem"). No key or sample explains it. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 19 | Render a grouped tafsir | The Resource says which ayahs share a commentary and how it should be shown | 18 entries cover a run of ayahs through `ayah_keys` and nothing documents how to display one. A run can also straddle a page boundary (37:167-170 splits across two Spreads of seven), so the obvious "print it on the run's first ayah" leaves later ayahs with no commentary on a page that never shows the first. Repeating the text on every ayah of the run with a "for ayahs N-M" label was the only self-contained option. | medium | 20 min | 1 |
| 20 | Render the tafsir text | Uniform plain text, or a plain-text variant like the translation's "simple" | Ten of 6236 entries wrap their whole text in a `<p>` element and the other 6226 do not. The tafsir offers no plain-text download variant and no catalog page or download note says the field may contain markup, so every consumer has to either carry a sanitiser or strip tags on a field that is plain text 99.8% of the time. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
| 21 | Use the translation's "simple" variant | Footnotes removed cleanly | The variant drops the footnote markers entirely but keeps the translator's bracketed insertions in 3409 of 6236 entries ("[All] praise is [due] to Allāh"), so a reader sees editorial brackets with no indication of where a note was. Which of the eight variants keeps what is documented nowhere; the only way to compare them is to download several and diff. | low | 10 min | 1 |
| 22 | Size the Facing Page for a "brief" tafsir | Short paragraphs, as "Al-Mukhtasar" (abridged) implies | Entries run from 18 to 2088 characters, median 235. One ayah's commentary can be nine times another's, so the Facing Page and Reading Page of a Spread can end up very different lengths. No length or size statistics are published for any Resource. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 23 | Confirm translation and tafsir cover every ayah | A stated ayah count on each Resource page | Neither catalog page says how many ayahs its file holds or whether coverage is complete. That both hold all 6236 keys, matching the script Resource, had to be verified with a script before the Facing Page could assume every ayah has a translation and a tafsir row. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 24 | Pick which timestamps to highlight by | The word-level `segments` triples and the ayah's `timestamp_from`/`timestamp_to` agree | They do not. The first word starts on average 530 ms after `timestamp_from` (up to 5 s late, and before it on 54 ayahs), and on 114 ayahs the last word's end differs from `timestamp_to` — 69:2's last word ends at 51297027 ms, fourteen hours into a seven-minute surah, and 28:3's ends 16 s past the ayah. Nothing says which pair is authoritative, so an ayah-level player has to guess (this one uses the ayah bounds). | medium | 25 min | 1 |
| 25 | Follow the reciter across a Spread | Ayah ranges tile the audio with no holes or overlaps | Neither. There is a 200 ms gap between ayahs on 5937 pairs, exactly 0 on two (52:38, 69:2), and 30 pairs overlap by up to 2.2 s. A naive "which range contains this time" lookup blinks the highlight off in every gap and returns two ayahs in every overlap, so the tie-break and the hold-through-the-gap rule have to be invented and documented by the Builder. | medium | 20 min | 1 |
| 26 | Play a Spread from the middle of a surah | Fetch the surah mp3 and seek | Works, but only because `audio-cdn.tarteel.ai` happens to serve byte ranges: Al-Baqarah is one 2-hour file, so a Spread 3000 s in is unplayable without them. Nothing in the recitation docs mentions file sizes, ranges, or that surah-by-surah means one file per surah rather than one per ayah. | low | 10 min | 1 |
| 27 | Read the audio through the Web Audio API | Set `crossorigin="anonymous"` like any CDN media | The CDN sends no `access-control-allow-origin` header at all, so a `crossOrigin` attribute kills playback outright and anything needing sample access (waveforms, tajweed feedback, a visualiser) is impossible against the hosted files. Not stated anywhere; the recitation tutorial's advice to self-host is the only hint. | medium | 15 min | 2 |
| 28 | Mix the recitation files with the metadata | One unit system across the recitation Resource | `surah.json`'s `duration` is seconds, every field in `segments.json` is milliseconds, and each ayah carries both `duration_sec` and `duration_ms`. No field name or doc says which is which; it is inferred from magnitudes. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 29 | Recite a Spread that shows the bismillah | The audio recites what the page shows | The bismillah line rendered above ayah 1 of surahs 2 to 114 is never recited: those audio files start on ayah 1 at 0 ms. The page therefore shows a line that the highlight can never reach, which has to be explained in the UI or accepted. | low | 5 min | 1 |
| 30 | Work out who to credit for each Resource | A producer or source line on each Resource page | No Resource page names who made the data or how to credit it. The only attribution anywhere is a site-wide `/credits` page that thanks contributors in prose ("Ayman Siddiqui — for his amazing work on Indopak and tajweed fonts and script", Tanzil, EveryAyah, King Fahd Quran Printing Complex) without saying which of the 500-plus Resources each one produced. Mapping a Resource to its contributor is guesswork, so the footer credits QUL and the catalog page number instead of the people who did the work. | medium | 20 min | 1 |
| 31 | Name each Resource in the attribution footer | One canonical display name per Resource | A Resource's name differs by where you read it: the catalog list, the page heading, and the page's og:title disagree (recitation 411 is "Mishari Rashid al-`Afasy, Surah by Surah" in the list and "Mishari Rashid al-`Afasy - recitation(Surah by Surah)" in its og:title), and the transliteration is "English Transliteration(Tajweed)" with no space. No downloaded file carries a name field, so the display name has to be transcribed by hand from the page and can silently drift from what QUL shows. The catalog path (`transliteration/469`) is the only stable identifier, so the footer shows that too. | low | 10 min | 1 |
| 32 | Decide whether the bundled data can ship in a public MIT repo and a Docker image | The license position answers it | It cannot, because there is no license position. Unstated licensing does not just mean "check later": it means a Builder cannot tell whether committing the JSON to a public repo, or baking it into a container, is permitted at all. The only legal document QUL links is Tarteel's product terms of use, which is about the Tarteel app and not about the datasets. The repo's MIT license therefore has to be carved out to cover the code only, with a README note telling anyone who forks it to ask Tarteel first. | medium | 15 min | 2 |
| 33 | Redistribute the Indopak font in the image | A font licence in the download, as almost every font ships | The font download is binaries only — no OFL, no licence text, no readme. Fonts are the most licence-sensitive asset here (`/credits` names both Ayman Siddiqui and the King Fahd Quran Printing Complex without saying which font came from which, and KFQPC assets normally carry usage restrictions), yet the font Resource page says less about rights than a typical Google Fonts page. Shipping it inside a container is a bet. | medium | 10 min | 2 |
| 34 | Follow the "self-host the audio" guidance | The instruction sits where the audio URL is handed to you | It does not. `surah.json` gives an `audio_url` pointing straight at `audio-cdn.tarteel.ai`, and neither that file, nor the recitation Resource page, nor the download notes mention self-hosting; the advice lives only in a recitation tutorial a Builder has to already know to open. Nor is the cost of complying published anywhere: no total or per-surah size is given for a reciter's audio, and Al-Baqarah alone turns out to be 116 MB, so a Builder cannot decide whether to self-host without downloading first to find out. | medium | 10 min | 1 |
